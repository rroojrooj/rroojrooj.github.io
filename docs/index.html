Why I'm building it

I started investing at 17, before I had my own brokerage account, so I bought my first stocks through my mum's. My first two books were Buffettology and The Intelligent Investor. They shaped how I think about wealth, business and investing, and changed my life. I owe a lot to Warren Buffett and the investors behind those ideas.

Investing that way takes real work: finding ideas, researching companies, deciding, then keeping up after I buy. It takes far more time than it should. Funds have the technology and teams to make it easier. Individual investors usually don't, and they deserve the same. That's why I'm building Belfort: fund-level support for anyone who invests seriously, without the price tag.

When I started in September 2025, I knew the problem but not how to build the answer. So the first months were learning: how to build a product from scratch, how LLMs and AI infrastructure work, which developer tools to use, UI and UX, modular and generic system design, agentic systems. And taste: telling a considered design choice from a sloppy one. Almost a year of how-tos.

Bristol Blockchain Society

I started forming it in 2021, in my foundation year, and it became official in September 2022. Few people backed it. Say “blockchain” and people heard “crypto”; more than one told me to focus on my studies. I believed blockchain could make finance permissionless, so I kept going.

We started with no members and no events. It grew into a weekly rhythm: a workshop on one topic (decentralised exchanges, Solidity, governance, money markets), then the pub, with food and drinks on the society — my favourite part. We brought in speakers from established companies and built relationships with Coinbase, Aave, Chainlink and the dYdX Foundation. I also set up a governance arm that researched proposals and voted as a student-run delegate, mostly on Arbitrum. At its peak it held over $300k in delegated voting power and raised more than £30,000 for the society.

I'm proudest of how far it came, and of the warm community it became.
